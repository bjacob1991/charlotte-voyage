## PAGE 39 (page-01)

### Thurs Nov 14, [200-] — [year digit partly cut off; context = 2002]
Whew! A long windy day! Daysails are very strenuous. Left Tutukaka at 6 am, for the 55-mile haul to Kawau Island. Saw some huge dolphins — big ones 6-8 feet long. Saw whales yesterday. It's pretty sailing along NZ's east coast, but you can't really enjoy it, it's so cold. Very little habitation along the coast line. We had some good sailing at times, over 7 knots to windward for a while. We are in a tiny, protected harbor, only a few local boats moored. Happy that we were able to sail the whole way, and average 5 knots.

### Fri Nov 15
Peaceful night in North Cove. Off again in gray morning to Gulf Harbour Marina — 10 miles sail to tip of peninsula. We are on the dock now with the cold south wind blowing on the boat, not very comfortable. Brian is thrilled, I am not. Hope it pays off.

### [day marker unclear in scan] Dec 29 — [calendar indicates Sunday]
Sailing again — it feels good! We spent 6 weeks in Gulf Harbour, didn't like it too much. The hot showers & internet access was good, but we hated the public showers. I worked for Dave Mackay, doing a website and then spent 6 days polishing some motor yacht. No fun cruising boats in Gulf Harbour, no kids for Brian to play with. We spent an evening with the Mackays (Flying Cloud) once a week, Brian spent the night several times. Went into Auckland one night for dinner, saw all the America's Cup boats at the Viaduct. Rented DVD's, watched a lot of movies, saw 2nd Harry Potter & Lord of the Rings on the big screen. Once I was done with work, we moved to Murrays Bay where *[entry continues on PAGE 40]*

## PAGE 40 (page-02)

*[continued from PAGE 39 — Dec 29 entry]*
Trevor & Leslie, (father we met in Tonga), live with their 4 kids, Jacob, Julia, Jasmine, & Jeremy (Cox, 70 Gulf View Dr). They are a great family. We had Christmas dinner at their house, Jake & Julia spent a lot of time on our boat. They took us for the day to Anawhata Beach on the west side, just 30 minutes from Auckland. The sand was black, the surf was white. Now we are moving north, to find warmer weather perhaps? Tried to get to Great Barrier Island, but the wind turned NE, so now we are at Bon Accord harbor on Kawau. Lots of boats here.

### Mon Dec 30, 2002
Kawau was ugly and crowded, didn't care for it there. Left this morning with a brisk east wind, but of course it didn't last. We are rolling along at a very slow pace. Otherwise it is a beautiful warm! sunny day. We will try to anchor at the entrance to Whangarei tonight.

### Wed Jan 15, 2003
We loved Urquharts Bay, spent 5 days there, hiking and windsurfing. Ocean Beach on the other side was beautiful and we hiked all over Bream Head. Had a nice New Year's Eve, made potato gnocchi and drank wine. Then we went up to Whangarei for a week. Ugly nasty dirty there but we got a lot accomplished — *[entry continues on PAGE 41]*

## PAGE 41 (page-03)

*[continued from PAGE 40 — Jan 15 entry]*
rebuilt the anchor winch, got an oil pressure gauge — finally! Left Monday, came back to Urquharts Bay. Today we sailed north to Whangamumu. Very rolly, Brian & Thomas are seasick, I am kind of green too. But we had a nice sail and Whangamumu harbor is very picturesque, surrounded by cliffs. The remains of an old whaling station are here. No town, no houses!

### Sat Jan 18
More great hiking at Whangamumu — we we[re] exhausted yesterday after hiking to some coves with a sandy beach. There were birds singing every morning, very nice. The water was clear. Now we are at Omakiwi Cove, no other boats here, a pretty stand of piney trees behind a sandy beach. Great view to the west of the Bay of Islands, boats moving around. We enjoyed a close-up view of Cape Brett as we rounded the point.

### Tues May 13
3½ months sitting in Opua! Thomas hurt himself windsurfing at Urupukapuka — the prettiest spot in the Bay of Islands. You could actually see the bottom there! We hung out in Opua so he could go to the doctor. Big strain in his hip joint — only time could cure it. Then I got another job doing web pages for Bob & Masae Seige, the Bay of Islands Travel Information Network. But we are finally on our way to South Minerva Reef. Nice 15 kt southerly breeze, but it is cold! Goodbye New Zealand — it was an interesting time!

## PAGE 42 (page-04)

### Wed May 14
Last night was beautiful, full moon, light SE winds, the boat roaring along at 6 knots! But of course it didn't last and now we are getting ready to tack, 3pm and the wind turned NE. Oh well, we knew it wouldn't be easy. Thomas has been seasick all day so I have had to do the deck work. Well we just tacked and of course the wind turned back SE, so we tacked again. Forgot what a pain in the ass sailing is! Anyway, last 24-hour run — 135 m[iles].

We had fun in Opua. Met Sharon & Steve on Poet's Place again, I took a lot of long walks with her and got to know her. Saw Infidean again briefly, Athenor showed up the last 2 weeks we were there. Really enjoyed Capaz — Rob, Sharin, and their 3 boys Brian, Scott, and Chris. Of course Brian became big buddies with them. I got into fixing laptop computers for the German/Swiss sailors and was a lot busier than I really cared to be. Leo on Moonlight was there, took us to Whangarei once, Rolf on Yelo let us borrow his car several times. But Sharon was the most generous, always taking people along with her. Finally met George & Lorraine on Silent Runner, who we had seen in Bora Bora. Sharon organized lots of pot luck movie nights at the Opua Cruising Club which were big fun. That was our main entertainment while we were there. I got to surf the net every day at work and bought a digital camera and another laptop on EBay. Thomas worked on the boat, time passed, and it got colder.

## PAGE 43 (page-05)

### Thurs May 15, 2003
It was a boisterous night, had to put in reefs, set the yankee jib. Now we're banging along, relatively comfortably, as long as we lie down! 30 knots of wind, close reaching, heading just to windward of our course. Everyone is over their seasickness. 24-hour run — 113 miles. But the sun is shining, the squalls are through, it's just trade winds.

### Fri May 16
Can't say this is comfortable but we are sailing in the right direction, still making easting from the rhumb line, anticipating a NE wind shift. Squally weather, 25-30 SE, making around 5 knots. We spent most of yesterday sleeping, not much else to do. Brian ended up on the floor last night after fighting with his bed and losing. Charlotte is happy with just a reefed mizzen and yankee — we sure are glad to have the 2nd forestay with the fitting we had welded to the bowsprit in Opua. The sail is almost new and flies beautifully. Brian is a good sailor, he takes the boredom, bland food, discomfort, with cheer. 24-hour run — 125 miles.

### Sat May 17
The wind keeps slowly turning north — kind of frustrating. We can handle the discomfort, the wetness, but to have to heave to will be a real bummer. Presently we are still slogging along, just barely on course. At least we got to shower and wash our hair. Except for Brian, who vows not to wash until we reach port! Charlotte is doing okay with staysail, double-reefed main and mizzen. Intermittent sun, not too squally yet. 24-hr run — 138 miles.

## PAGE 44 (page-06)

### Sun May 18
Wind turned NE last night, we are making very little easting now. Will need a favorable wind shift to get to South Minerva. Woke up to 30-35, very rough. Got the main down, been riding relatively comfortably with the boat closed up, no looking around today! We are all reading, nothing to do but keep still. Oh isn't it fun! 24-hr run — 125 miles.

### Mon May 19
It's only gotten worse — total cloud cover, squally all last night and today, put another reef in the mizzen. The boat is not slamming so much now. We are still heading due north, so will miss Minerva Reef. Too bad — Capaz made it in this morning, we would like to be comfortably anchored! Instead we will have to continue on to Fiji. All the other boats are hove to. It's not really that bad! Heavy weather is boring. I had to sew the bimini top — a seam had come undone. We got Brian to wash a little bit. 24 hour run — 100 miles.

### Tues May 20
The gnarly weather is finally through! The squalls were less violent and shorter last night and this morning we were able to set the yankee. Passed South Minerva reef this morning, 35 miles to the west, and talked to Gene on Reflections. So we will carry on to Savu Savu. Heading due north, some sunshine today, hardly any squalls. It was nice to talk to someone else, knew after being alone out here for a week. I took a nice rainwater shower this morning — it is much warmer. Back to shorts and T shirts. It would have been nice to stop though, the boat is an awful mess. 24-hr run 110 miles.

## PAGE 45 (page-07)

### Wed May 21
Still squally, main up and main down, but the sun is out and we seem to be through the worst of the front. Talked to Capaz this morning, the boys were missing Brian. We wanted to stop so bad, have time to clean up, get ready for Fiji, oh well. The boat is an absolute slime pit. But it is hot! We are lolling around in no shirts. Every towel on the boat is wet with sea water. And we'll get there on the weekend and have to hide from customs til Monday. Seems like I wrote the same thing yesterday! Got an email from Sharon — Poet's Place — about how awful the 30-hour flight back to Seattle was — little does she know! 24-hr run 120 miles. Wind 20 knots, seas 3 meter swell, maintaining 5 knots to windward still — Charlotte can handle this!

### Thurs May 22
Land ho! We spotted the first Fijian island a while ago. We will pass it by sunset, then sail between islands all night. Will arrive in Savu Savu tomorrow sundown. It's a nice day, 15-20 knots, lots of sun, still going into it, but the big ocean swell is gone — there are islands way to windward. We are happy with Charlotte — maintaining 5 knots to windward in big seas! 24-hour run — 135 miles.

### Fri May 23
Good day! Thought we would have a nice calm sail last night, wind and seas were down, had full sails up. Caught a 4-ft Mahi mahi just before sunset. Brian got it in with the new gaff — easier than *[entry continues on PAGE 46]*

On the water is the place to be in Fiji, as the air is hot. Will take some getting used to. Everyone is happy with a good successful voyage complete. It's great to be cruising again!

But like all unpleasant situations, it came to an end, and now we are alone, with Capaz, outside Matagi Island in a small cove on the north shore. Nice snorkeling right in the anchorage. Caught a wahoo coming in and had a barbeque on Capaz last night. Very green, smells earthy here.

## PAGE 49 (page-11)

### Tuesday June 24
It piped up at sundown last night — up to 28 knots according to Rob. We had to reef down but Charlotte was flying — over 7 knots, must have been current. The seas were very confused. No sleeping — too many noises! Sailed into the Great Astrolabe Reef — about 4pm. It sure is nice here — lots of beaches! The locals remember Capaz and are happy to see them. I had a remora try to attach to me when I went swimming — yuck! We did 145 miles in 24 hours.

### [day illegible] July 8 — [calendar indicates Tuesday]
So we have been 2 weeks in the Kadavu — Astrolabe Reef area. Rob organized a pig roast at Dravuni last week that was great fun — 4 boats with kids, the extended family he knows there. We really enjoyed our time on Yaukuve, a deserted island with a nice sandy beach. The kids built a big hut out of palm fronds and spent 2 nights sleeping ashore. We had beach bonfires several nights. ### [day illegible] July 9 — [calendar indicates Wednesday]
What a ride to Suva! We were on a broad reach — 30 knots — reefed main and reefed mizzen, doing 7 knots. It was boisterous but over quickly. Now we are in the big city, spending money. Doesn't look that inviting but the fresh fruits & vegetables are good!

## PAGE 50 (page-12)

### Tues July 15
Time to get out of Suva — we spent lots of money, got what we needed — new printer for $50 US, a set of big wrenches for Thomas, new binoculars. We enjoyed the street food, loaded the boat with fruits & vegetables, saw Terminator III at the movie theater. Disappointment was not meeting any new people. Royal Suva Yacht Club was a bore. Got hit with more wind than we expected today, on a 30-mile sail out to Beqa. Wind kept picking up, under bare poles for a while! At least it is off the wind. Anchored in the harbor, feeling extremely wind blown, finally got the anchor to stick. It is very gusty here but there is nowhere else to go. Caught a big golden Dorado!

### Thurs July 17
Out of that hole! We were heeled over half the time at anchor, gusts up to 50 knots, no fun at all. I did get in a swim yesterday, between rain squalls. Would have liked to visit the village, but it was too windy to launch the dinghy. It gusted 40 or more as we pulled up the anchor, motored the 4 miles out to the reef. Once on course downwind, with just a staysail, it is quite comfortable. Heading for Vunaniu harbor, hoping for a calm anchorage for a change!

### Fri July 18
Well it was a very comfortable night, totally calm, not much wind, what a change! Today the sun is hot, the wind is light, from the north. We are sailing with 2 other boats who were in Suva and spent the night with us. Just puttering along, enjoying the sunshine. Will stop in Natadola harbor, looks like it has a nice long beach.

## PAGE 53 (page-15)

### Wed Aug 20
Happy birthday Thomas! 15 knots headwinds and we are tacking, bashing into the seas and making very little progress. Charlotte is not a particularly happy boat today. Otherwise the weather is fine, nice sunny day. Lost two fish, one a nice mahi. Guess we'll be out here forever.

### Thurs Aug 21
Well we did about 40 miles yesterday, tacking nonstop. Got tired of it this morning, winds are lighter, can't hold a decent course, extremely frustrated. Did catch a big bull dolphin this morning, one bright spot in the day! Finally broke down and tried motoring, but it's hopeless — only 3.5 knots made good. Don't understand how all these other people can motor for days on end. The wind is supposed to come up tomorrow — we can only hope.

### Fri Aug 22
The wind turned last night for 3 hours! We were so happy, the boat was sailing so nice! It did finally come to the south this morning and we sailed slowly, watching Efate get bigger. Great day, lots of sunshine. We will be in just after dark tonight. Everyone is happier, anticipating landfall. It was a long trip.

### Mon Sept 1
A quick week in Vila as usual. Anchored at 8pm near the quarantine buoy — the map was right on. Silent Runner didn't show up for two days — their transmission had broken again and they hove to, rather than tacked. Guess we didn't do so bad after all. Lots of people here we know — Rolf with Doniella on Yelo and best of all Desert Rose with Mick, Lynne, Teddo, Edwina & Issy. We hung out with them a year ago in Tonga. Had some fun evenings with them.
