**Conventions (same as earlier batches):**

-   `[illegible; note]` = cannot be read.
-   `~~word~~` = struck through in the original. `^word^` = inserted above or below the line.
-   `*[...]*` = transcriber note, not part of the log.
-   Spelling, capitalization and "&" follow the handwriting; slips are marked *[sic]* rather than corrected.
-   Entries that run across pages are marked at the bottom of one page and the top of the next.

### Thurs Nov 11 (continuation)

*[continued from PAGE 99, "...The landscape was very stark, rolling hills & plains with few large trees. It is"]* extremely dry and the animals were searching for water. We had a wonderful day, hanging out of the van taking pictures. We drove through some spread-out Zulu villages and saw their typical architecture — one round grass thatched building for sleeping, 2 other small square buildings for storage, etc. The children were spread out for miles along the roads, walking to school. White people are all very scared and we can't walk anywhere — everything in cars. What a hassle. We moved to a mooring at the Zululand Yacht Club & passed 5 peaceful days there — I could even get swim! But we have southeast winds today so we are out of here. Bedouin, Just Jinx, & Golden Sovereign *[confirms the "Golden Sovereign" reading on Pg90]* arrived from the Mozambique channel and already headed on. Sterling and a South African boat, Kiren, are sailing with us. Also got to see Pierre on Quo Vadis again. Brian spent a couple of evenings with Shawn, that was good.

### Fri Nov 12

Very uncomfortable night, waves from every direction but the wind held and we approached Durban after sunrise. Huge skyline. I think this is the biggest city we have ever sailed to. Harbor is nasty but the city has everything we need, including a new boom! Dos Tintos is here.

### Sat Nov 27

Maybe this time we will succeed in leaving Durban! We left Tuesday but turned around after 24 hours when the wind turned SW. Finally met the Japanese family on Akitsushima and had Pierre on Quo Vadis & Dietmar on Anke 2 over for *[entry continues on PAGE 101]*

### Sat Nov 27 (continuation)

*[continued from PAGE 100]* dinner. This time we waited until the wind had definitely turned east before we left. A bit of motoring, but we finally caught the Agulhas current before sundown. Thanks to Tintos who told us where it was! We are happy to be out of Durban with the nasty water. We did go to the beach and craft market one day — bought Zulu baskets, walking sticks and a massive carved rhino to remind us of our experience in Hluhluwe game park. Met Fred the net guy and his wife Eva and had lunch at their house. Replaced the broken boom, fixed some other stuff. So it was a useful stop but the city was big, scary and dirty. Could only walk within a 5-block radius of the marina. People sleeping in the streets.

### [uncertain: Sun] Nov 28

Today is Lynne's 50th birthday — she did not want to be reminded of it! Don't blame her, being over 50 is no fun. Had a great ride last night — speeds up to 11.7 and averaged 10 knots for hours. Winds picked up a bit for a while but always from the east. Hard to keep in the current, especially farther south where it is narrower. We are doing almost 10 now, with 10 knots from the east and flat seas — quite nice! Should be in East London sometime after dark, hope Tintos will be around to help us. 240 miles in less than 36 hours! What a ride. Saw whales jumping yesterday when we left, none today.

## PAGE 102 (p03)

### Thurs Dec 2 (continuation)

*[continued from PAGE 101]* a braai was planned for tonight at their house, we hated to miss it, but we know you have to go when the wind blows. Saw Chris from Bedouin briefly, took a walk to the beach — beautiful sand dunes. The highlight was meeting Anna again after 20 years! We had met her, her 2 sets of twins, & Scottish husband in Las Olas anchorage, Ft. Lauderdale, when we were on Wanderly. She had wanted to take Salty but couldn't for some reason. She invited me to do yoga with her the other morning. She is now alone on a steel boat she built herself. We really enjoyed seeing her again. So now we have 10 knots SE, light seas, beam reaching between the N & S-bound shipping lanes. It will be a sleepless night.

### Fri Dec 3, 2004

Sunshine, sailing downwind in flat water, sand dunes on the beach. Gwendolyn & Dos Tintos are already in Port Elizabeth. It was a quiet night. Will be tied up to the wall this afternoon.

### Wed Dec 8

Port Elizabeth was a real dump. Had to motorsail the last 20 miles and we hated it when we arrived. Did find a dock space that Craig on Gwendolyn had arranged. He & Tintos spent all their time in the yacht club bar of course. Got rained in for 2 days, absolutely nothing to do but walk to the grocery store and have a cheap ice cream. We left early this morning, hoping for a good southerly breeze. We are now sailing west, just south of 34° S. The wind is painfully light, motored for 7 hours, now barely moving. How we love South Africa! It did improve! Sailing at 3–4 knots, not so bad. Coastline is getting more interesting.

## PAGE 104 (p05)

### Fri Dec 31, 2004

On the road again — first New Years at sea. Left Capetown, finally, yesterday. Got stuck waiting for Brian's Christmas present to arrive. Spent Christmas at the yacht club, Steve & Julie on Dos Tintos organized a barbeque. It lasted well into the night, full moon, some people got really drunk. We enjoyed meeting Alana & Dany on Thai and did some hiking with them up Lions Peak and Table Mountain. That almost killed us, even Brian was tired and happy to take the cable car back down. Great view up there, fun to get away from the harbor and out in the green. Also met Steve the Kiwi on Long White Cloud. Stella Maris & Quo Vadis showed up. But we decided to forego the New Years Eve party and get going across the Atlantic instead. Yesterday was very slow, mostly becalmed for 12 hours in front of Table Mountain. Wind picked up steadily at night and by 8 am we were reefing. Wind from SW and big westerly swell keeps throwing us around. No celebration tonight — too rough! But Brian is content to play his new Game Boy games so it doesn't matter. We are very happy to be done with Africa! 24-hour run — 99 miles

### Sat Jan 1, 2005

Happy New Year! It was a boisterous one — must have been blowing at least 30 when the new year rolled around. Had to take the main down until morning. Still blowing S 25, we are poled out, averaging 7 knots. A bit more comfortable than last night but still cold! Showers are no fun. Started school today — oh joy. Everyone in a good mood, glad to be away from Africa. 24-hr run: 148 miles

### [uncertain: Mon] Jan 17

What a perfect day to leave on a journey! We enjoyed St. Helena so much, we decided to stay the weekend. We took a tour of the island and saw its lush interior with green valleys. Learned more than we cared to know about Napoleon's exile, and had lunch on his tomb. I had a long swim in the big pool. The outside of the island was rocky & barren, but the interior is a hidden gem. The people were very friendly — we were the only visitors on the island as there is no airport. Our arrival was announced on the local radio station. The water was crystal clear, deep blue, we could see the bottom 50 feet down. Only drawback was the rolly anchorage. Today we are suffering from sore leg muscles, after climbing the 700 steps in Jacob's Ladder, a stairway to the top of the cliff on one side of the little city, Jamestown, which was in the crack of a valley. It was very quaint with European-style houses squeezed together. Next stop Fernando de Noronha, another 1730 miles.

### [uncertain: Tues] Jan 18

So much for perfect trade wind sailing. Wind got light last night, even lighter today. Managing to maintain 4 knots until now. Cirrus clouds down south. Oh well — lots of sunshine anyway! Catching up on our rest, rolling less than St. Helena anchorage! 24-hr run — 108 miles

### Sun Jan 23

Incredible to be ghosting along at 3.5–4 knots in this flat calm sea! How expectations can change — we are content to do 100 miles a day. I think this is the most enjoyable ocean voyage we have ever had — everyone is content with the moment, the 3 of us out here in the gentle sea. I am reading Desperate Voyage — his trip was totally different from ours. I would like to have kittens aboard too. Been running off the wind with poled-out genoa, main & mizzen, mizzen drifter up forward, but now we are reaching as the wind has turned NE. Can't seem to make any northing. On our uncomfortable tack, but it makes no difference in these benign conditions. 24-hr run — 102 miles

### Tues Jan 25

We have a shark swimming along next to the boat for hours now. First time we have ever seen this, although we have heard of it. At least it looks like a shark — it's not a dolphin. Got some wind today, not steady, but we are doing 5 at times. Then it drops away and we wallow. Brian enjoys taking noon sights, although he hates the math. Life proceeds at a calm pace out here. 24-hr runs — 73 & 104 miles

### Thurs Jan 27

Time passes — we keep hoping the wind will pick up & stay up, but it never does. Had a colorful full moonrise 2 nights ago. It was blood red. Thomas wants to eat him of course. Also have 2 small stripey fish who have been with us a long time. Lots of sunshine, lots of rolling, but we move on. 24-hr runs: 94 & 91

### Sun Jan 30

What a great night — no banging of sails, slamming of main boom. Wind held up all night, did 5 knots. Today down to 4 again but it's better than 3! Getting hotter every day. Lost our yellowfin after Thomas made a harpoon. But we now have 3 stripey fish with us. 24-hr runs: 91, 84, & 101

### Thurs Mar 31

Our companion and friend the Southern Cross has finally left us after 3 years in the night sky. We miss it. The wind is frustratingly light and we are sailing only 3–4 knots. So it will take us 2 days to get to Georgetown. We cross our track today, south of Rum Cay.

### Fri Apr 1

April Fools Day and we enter Georgetown harbor, 3 years, 3 months to the day since we left! Fine night, we rounded Cape Santa Maria at midnight, and the Southern Cross was still in the sky! Had to slow down as the wind had picked up, ghosted along until sunrise. Entered at the south end and found Tiama with Helmut & Pauline, and Lalaube with Reiner & Renate, in Red Shanks. What an exquisite little pond to anchor in! The colors are beautiful. We are happy to see Helmut & Pauline again. Now the circumnavigation is officially over!

### Tues April 5

Wonderful 4 days in Georgetown, too short. Loved seeing Helmut & Pauline again, they never change, just Tiama is more ragged. Helmut spends all his time messing around on the computer rather than fixing his boat! Brian & Thomas got in some good windsurfing. Reiner & Renate on Lalaube were there also, the big Joshua so neat. Went up the hill yesterday to visit Tacelopes again — Ralf, Bergit, John & Pascal. We anchored in Red Shanks which was peaceful, away from the crowds. The town was crawling with American boaters, we are not used to seeing so many. It was such a fun time, we would have liked to stay longer. But the wind was east this morning, time to push north again. Had a screaming sail in Exuma Sound, up to Cave Cay cut. Now we are taking it easy, recovering; stayed up until midnight drinking wine on Helmut's boat. Really enjoy those folks. Thomas caught a big dorado just as we left the blue water.

### [uncertain: Wed] Apr 6

Got going at 8 am, crossed over the shallow banks by Galliot and out onto the Exuma Banks. Great sailing, fast & comfortable with no seas on a reach. ### [uncertain: Thurs] Apr 7

Reached the Tongue of the Ocean before sunset, turned NW to sail past New Providence & up to the Berrys. Lots of wind, never dropped below 7 knots, got a bit rolly once we were past New Providence. Dawn found us off the Berrys, made Great Stirrup by 11 am. Had to abort our attempt to enter the anchorage, tide was running out, wind was blowing on the shoals, and the channel was very narrow, with 2 boats already there. So we went across to Goat Cay, a bit lively but lots of swinging room. Too bad we couldn't keep going, but there's a cold front coming and no other good anchorages past here. We did enjoy the past 24 hours sailing!

### [uncertain: Mon] Apr 11

Three days went by quickly in Great Harbor. We didn't do much — had a day of blustery wind & rain, a day of sun & wind where Brian had a great windsurfing session, a day of waiting for the wind to turn. Left at sunrise this morning with light NE winds and they died completely. Been motoring almost all day — just passing Freeport Grand Bahama, now, just before sunset. Hoping the wind will come up by dark. The water is incredibly clear and blue.

### [uncertain: Tues] Apr 12

Yeah — we did get wind when the sun went down! It wasn't much but we were able to flop and bang along all night. Now we are in the middle of the Gulf Stream, doing 4–5 knots with about that much wind. It will put us in Cape Canaveral at sunrise. Too bad our last sail has to be such an obnoxious one, tired of flapping sails. Oh well — Had plenty of this around the world!
