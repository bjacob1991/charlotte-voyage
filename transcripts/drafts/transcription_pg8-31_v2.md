# Charlotte — Logbook Transcription (Scan: Pg8–Pg31) — Revised

Covers the passage from Port Antonio, Jamaica through the San Blas Islands, Portobelo, the Panama Canal, Las Perlas, the Galápagos, the Marquesas, the Tuamotus, Tahiti and the Society Islands, on to the Cook Islands (bypassed), Niue, Tonga, Minerva Reef, and landfall in New Zealand. Spans Jan 18 through (approximately) Nov 13, 2002.

**Conventions:** transcribed verbatim, preserving original voice and spelling. Uncertain readings in `[brackets]`; wholly unreadable words `[illegible]`.
**This revision adds:** verified place-name spellings (checked against geographic sources), stay-duration notes where the log shows a gap of more than a couple of days at one location (shown as `▸ STAY: …`), and region/country/island-chain departure markers (`◆ DEPARTURE: …`).

## Leg 02 — Caribbean & Panama

### Port Antonio, Jamaica (departing)

▸ STAY: One week in Port Antonio, Jamaica.

**Thurs Jan 18**
One week in Port Antonio — it was enough, even though the beer was cold, the fresh food delicious, the surroundings lush. The place has become very run down. Only a few boats in the harbor. We took a raft trip on the Rio Grande river — poled on a bamboo raft. It was nice to see the countryside. It is a good harbor — we weren't quite ready for the waves and swells outside. Beat to windward in 10–15 knots along the coast — kind of bumpy. Will bear off when we pass Morant Point. Charlotte is flying! The akee and breadfruit we had for dinner last night was excellent, along with Dragon Stout. Bound for the San Blas Islands.

◆ DEPARTURE: Leaving Jamaica / the Greater Antilles, bound SW for the San Blas Islands (Guna Yala), Panama — a ~550 nm passage.

**Fri Jan 18** *(as written; sequence Thu–Fri–Sat suggests this should likely read Jan 17)*
Since we had forgotten, we got a rude reminder of the Caribbean wind and seas! Nice sailing last night — beautiful stars, broad reach in 15 knots, until we went under the squall line. Then bam — wind turned ESE, picked up to 25–30, and stayed there all day. Down to reefed mizzen and deeply reefed genoa. Not what we had hoped for. All we can do is lie around and try to eat. Brian won't leave the aft cabin. The sea is pretty though, with all the foaming waves. When the boat goes over 7–7.5 knots, the motion gets violent and we have to shorten sail.

**Sat Jan 19**
It's rough, you can't be outside without taking a shower. Beautiful sunny weather though! Speed 4.5–5.5 knots, with same sail — reefed mizzen and reefed genoa. Can't do anything. Brian is feeling better today, still not eating much. The clew lines broke today.

**[Sun] Jan 20, 2002**
[Even] more wind this morning — 30–35 knots, big seas. Since we [only] have 200 miles to go, we decided to slow the boat down so we arrive Tuesday morning. Reefed the genoa down to handkerchief size and put a second reef in the mizzen. Still taking occasional breakers over the boat. It's impossible to be on deck without getting soaked. There is nothing to do but lie around. Sailing is so much fun!

**Mon Jan 21**
A nicer day altogether! The wind turned NE last night so we are off the wind. Brian is his usual self again! It's down to 20+ today — sailing along with the same tiny sail rig at 4 knots so we will arrive at San Blas tomorrow after sunrise. We were able to be on deck today and enjoy the fresh air. Took a big breaker into the boat this morning — soaked the chart table — got to do something about that. We have learned a lot about Charlotte and sailing in wind on this trip, although the lesson has been uncomfortable!

### San Blas Islands (Guna Yala), Panama — Holandes Cays, Green Island, Chichimé

▸ STAY: ~11 days in the San Blas Islands (Jan 22 – Feb 2), anchoring at Holandes Cays, Green Island, and Chichimé.

**Tues Jan 22**
Beautiful landfall, after a long uncomfortable rolly night. We saw a lot of ships at one point, must have been crossing the Canal shipping lane. Finally hoisted the main again as we approached Holandes Cays. Decided that weather cloths will help keep water out of the boat. It's beautiful here — our first landfall in a new place — finally breaking new ground with Charlotte! It is very sunny and the sun is hot. Water is all colors of blue — islands covered with palm trees. 550 mile sail — 4½ days.

**Sat Feb 2**
Enjoyed our stay in the San Blas islands — anchored at Holandes Cays, Green Island, and Chichimé. Good windsurfing at Green Island. Bought some molas, enjoyed the Kuna Indians — very gentle and friendly. They had very nice dugouts, sailed some of them. The islands were green, palm-covered, the water blue like the Bahamas, beautiful reefs, but very few fish. Met the Austrian boat Idemo with Robert, Ingrid and 5-yr Anna aboard. Had some pleasant day sails around the islands. The weather was overcast — more towards the mainland. On the outer islands, the reefs crashed constantly and everything was salty.

◆ DEPARTURE: Leaving the San Blas, running W along Panama's Caribbean coast to Portobelo.

### Portobelo, Panama

**Sat Feb 2 (cont.)**
Left early this morning for Portobelo. 3 other boats left too. It was a superhighway along Panama's north coast! Boisterous sailing, 20–25 knots, beam reach, reefed genoa, reefed mizzen, double-reefed main. We kept up pretty well with the bigger boats. Finally got to see the green hills of the Panama coast towards the end of the trip. Coast is rocky, mostly deep to shore, but with numerous off-lying reefs. Not a place to sail at night! Portobelo is a beautiful natural harbor — plenty big for a fleet of Spanish galleons! 55 miles in 8½ hours, over 6½ knots average.

### Colón, Panama

▸ STAY: Stayed in/around Colón roughly two weeks (early Feb – Feb 19), including the Portobelo stop just prior and the wait for a canal transit slot.

**Wed Feb 6**
Downwind sail to Colón today — nice sail for a change, no waves on deck! The coastline is green and rugged. Entering the breakwater at Colón was exciting. Big nasty town. We loved Portobelo — most beautiful anchorage we have ever seen. Three forts guarding the entrance from all sides. We climbed to the top of one fort, took a short dinghy ride up a river. Peter's boat Tiamat was there — we met him in Brazil — but he had died two weeks before. We enjoyed the little town, felt comfortable standing around drinking beer. Now the real hustle begins.

**Tues Feb 19**
Well our stay in Colón was a lot more fun than we expected. It helped that we met some people! In particular, Kelcey, Jim, Chris, and Nicholas who is Brian's age. He was off at the yacht club every afternoon, so Thomas and I had a lot of time alone together, which was very nice. They bought a new outboard motor — 4 HP Yamaha. We enjoyed the street food of Colón, although it was a filthy city. Must have been nice at one point — many sidewalks were tiled, indications of past glory. It was easy to get around though, and people were friendly and helpful, proud to have our business for the most part. Transited the canal today, rafted to a French boat [Jonaia]. We had 3 American line handlers on board — only Ken, the boat owner, knew what he was doing. It was all right when the [French] boat was steering us along the tugs, as their pilot was complet[ely] [illegible]. But when Thomas had to do the steering, the wind and current rammed [us] into the side wall. Broke the stanchion Don had welded, scraped the side but no paint lost. We roared through Lake Gatún under motor and genoa, going as fast as we were able. It was very interesting, sailing through the middle of Panama! Had a wait a long time at the last locks, so we didn't clear them until after 6. It was 8 pm, completely dark, by the time we anchored at Flamenco Cay. What an experience! No need to repeat it soon.

◆ DEPARTURE: Transited the Panama Canal (Feb 19) — crossed from the Caribbean/Atlantic side to the Pacific side. Anchored at Flamenco (Isla Flamenco, Amador, Panama City).

### Flamenco / Panama City, Panama (Pacific side)

▸ STAY: ~1 week at Flamenco / Panama City (Feb 19 – Feb 27).

**Wed Feb 27**
Finally leaving the Panama Canal Zone! Left this morning from Flamenco Cay anchorage for Las Perlas. Light wind, but pleasant sailing at 3–4 knots, full sails, no rolling! After 3 weeks of rolling with pilot boat wakes, it is a pleasure. Panama City was BIG! Buses were confusing, taxis comfortable but more expensive. We got what we needed there — speargun, spare parts, more food and booze. I had my loose tooth pulled. The usual assortment of older Americans and European single-handers. Wind picked up later in the morning and we had a great sail in calm — Pacific! — waters, through the islands, to Contadora. Reef anchorage — 40 feet.

### Las Perlas, Panama — Contadora & Viveros

▸ STAY: ~2 weeks in the Las Perlas (Pearl Islands), Feb 27 – Mar 13, between Contadora and Isla Viveros, including Thomas's foot infection and treatment back at Contadora.

**Wed March 13**
Well Las Perlas was not exactly what we expected. Contadora was rolly and uncomfortable, so we moved 15 miles to Viveros Cay. Pretty anchorage, surrounded by beaches and little islands, but the murky water was disappointing — spearfishing out of the question. Then Thomas stepped on something in the shallow water and got the most horrific infection. He was in pain, couldn't walk for 3 days, and his foot kept getting worse. Meanwhile Brian and I enjoyed walks on the beaches at low tide and had fun scrambling around on the rocks. The shells were mostly scallops, purple and orange. Beautiful views across the islands from some shady rock under a tree. I really enjoyed the solitude there — no one but small local fishing boats, gorgeous skies at night. We finally went back to Contadora on Thurs Mar. 7 and luckily there was a doctor there. Thomas was in for 5 days of shots, heavy anti[biotics], and his foot slowly started to heal. Very nice young doctor — Cresmo [Garcia], who was interested in our plans and gave us some [medication] to take with us. We got used to touristy Contadora — really just a sleepy town — and had our routine of afternoon ice cream and beer at the local mini super. Very dry though — no water — I now wash what I can in sea water. We enjoyed our waiting time as best [we] could.

◆ DEPARTURE: Leaving Panama and the Americas behind — departing Las Perlas for the Galápagos, the start of the great Pacific crossing.

## Leg 03 — The South Pacific

### Las Perlas → Galápagos (passage)

**Wed March 13 (cont.)**
Now Thomas is almost completely healed and we are under way for the Galápagos! Left this morning, after a favorable wind report from boats on the way. We have been sailing past the Perlas all day, under full genoa and then spinnaker. 12 knots wind NNE, tiny seas, beautiful sailing, the boat is upright! Good omen this morning — we saw a perfect rainbow circle around the sun. Panama was a pleasant surprise, really enjoyed the scenery, the friendly people, the great food, the cheap beer! Could have stayed longer if that was our plan. For now, we are happy to be underway again, with a fair wind. The Pacific truly lives up to its name, at least at these latitudes!

**Thurs Mar 14**
Here we are, cruising along at 5.5 knots, under spinnaker and mizzen. Thomas is happy — no sail chafe, and he has always wanted to fly the mizzen drifter since it is so weird. It works great on a broad reach. Winds just turned more easterly and are filling in 6–8 knots. Last night we had an incredible ride — 8.5, surfing to [past] [illegible] for several hours in 20+ knots, broad reaching. The boat was [so] [illegible] it was almost scary, but the [Aries] steered well. The wind died down this morning for a while. All in all it has been very pleasant. [Lots] of sea life, not sure what we are seeing. First 24 hr run — 148 m.

**Fri Mar 15**
Ran under our funny rig until the middle of the night when the wind picked up. NE winds, 15–20, full sail, boomed out genoa, 5+ knots. Got a bit rolly for a while. Weather report is favorable, some wind tomorrow. All happy on board. 24 hr run — 117 m.

**Sat Mar 16**
Well so much for weather reports! Very light wind last night, banged and rolled, down to 2 knots. More of same today, slightly more wind. Spinnaker flying. It is comfortable when we don't roll — hardly know we are at sea. We are well rested, nothing going on at night. Put a spreader chafe patch on the main sail this morning. 24 hour run — 106 m.

**Sun Mar 17**
Another slow night, averaged 3.5 knots, but wind came up some in the morning. Now it is dying again. At least we continue to move! Spent the day working on a crew list in 3 languages. Brian is getting adept at the computer. The water is blue here, and we must have some current. 24 hours run — 97 m.

**Mon Mar 18**
It got worse. Totally becalmed for hours last night — we just slept. Wind came up at 3 am from the south so we took down the pole. 3–4 knots in 6 knots of wind — amazing Charlotte can move at all! Now we are motoring because Thomas is impatient. It's really horrible. 24 hour run — 75 [m].

**Wed Mar 20**
Three 24-hour run — [80] m — nothing more needs to be said. Can you believe — head winds! We alternate between sailing south or west and motoring. Big swell from the SW hampers our progress. Kind of frustrating. 24 hour run — 87 m.

### Galápagos — San Cristóbal (Puerto Baquerizo Moreno / Wreck Bay)

▸ STAY: ~11 days at San Cristóbal (Mar 22 – Apr 2), anchored in Wreck Bay off Puerto Baquerizo Moreno.

**[Fri] Mar 22, 2002**
Landfall this morning at sunrise! We motored all night in calm [seas]. It was a bummer, but we're here and San Cristóbal is beautiful. Dolphins playing, sea lions on the beaches, huge eagle rays lying on the surface, looking like sharks with double fins. Kicker Rock looks like a big foot. We dropped anchor in Wreck Bay around 11, a number of boats we saw in Panama are here. Everyone is friendly.

**Apr 1**
It has been a fun 10 days in Puerto Baquerizo Moreno — we really enjoyed the town and harbor. The sea lions were constant entertainment, swimming and playing around the boats, climbing up on every possible surface. We had to pick up the dinghy every night or they would have sunk it. We went on a land tour to El Junco and around with the Dutch boat Kenza and the [Danish-Brazilian] Sundowner II. Our taxi driver Carlos took us to a friend's [farm] where we picked fresh fruits and vegetables in the pouring [rain], using banana leaves over our heads like umbrellas. It was the highlight of the day — so peaceful in the fields, with the [illegible] singing and a view of the east coast of the island. We drank cheap beer at a panaderia and got to know all the local shopkeepers. Made friends with Steve & Sharon on Poet's Place and [flying] genoa on Candlewin. Visited the Interpretive Center and learned about the history of the Galápagos. We saw blue-footed boobies [diving] for fish behind the boat, lava lizards and marine iguanas basking in the sun on the lava rocks, very prehistoric looking. We were amazed [at] the lushness of the island — everything grows in the rich volcanic soil.

### Galápagos — Isabela (Puerto Villamil)

▸ STAY: ~12 days at Isabela (Apr 2 – Apr 14), anchored off Puerto Villamil.

**Tues Apr 2**
Weird night, drifting west with the current. Motored at sunrise, just went to get there. Went close to Isla Tortuga, sunken volcano crater, half moon out of water, cool to look at! Puerto Villamil at Isabela is beautiful, tucked between some lava islands and the shore, with sandy beaches and palm trees. The little islands contain all kinds of wild life — Galápagos penguins, small, black & white, very awkward and funny on land, blue-footed boobies with their unbelievable bright blue feet, marine iguanas looking like prehistoric monsters, and of course sea lions! There is a turtle hatchery a short walk away and a lagoon full of pink flamingos. Fascinating! Plus lots of boats, parties on the beach. We love it here!

**Sun Apr 14**
Left Puerto Villamil yesterday afternoon, after a false start. We were chased by the Port Captain and asked to return and check out properly! No big deal — he is really a nice guy. Hard to describe the wonderful time we had at Isabela. Athanor showed up with Beeca, 10 and Ollie 8, and their parents Eddie and Eileen. All the boats from Wreck Bay eventually pulled in. Attended a wild party at Jeff & Claudia's house, American and Argentinian living here with their kids. Saw all kinds of wild life. Went to the turtle hatchery and saw turtles from 1 month to 100 years old. Found a lagoon full of pink flamingos. Took a great horseback ride to the volcano — it was really neat, riding up the outside of the mountain, then walking on the lava fields down into the crater. It was like a moonscape — mostly black but other colors when the sun shone on it. Some deep clefts were still smoking, ferns growing in them. This one had erupted in 1969. We passed another crater on horseback that is the second largest volcanic crater in the world. Very interesting day. Brian went again, with the other kids. He had such a good time, playing all day on the beach. We barely saw him! One day we went dinghying with Jean and Sherry on Reflection, helped a sick tortoise that was covered with oysters. I thought he was dead at first. Brian and I saw him again 2 days later, swimming normally and able to dive. We got very close to some penguins. Another day I actually touched one that was sitting on a rock! They were all standing there, stretching their stubby wings, wearing their black and white tuxedos! Brian and I went on an early morning walk on the lava islands and saw hundreds of iguanas, almost stepped on them. Got into a routine of walking to town at 4 pm for a delicious homemade pastry fresh from the bakery and an ice cold beer. Decided not to go to Easter Island with Greg & genoa on Candlewin — we need to stay with Athanor for Brian. We will miss those guys though. It was hard to leave Isabela, with the incredible wildlife and the renewed social life. But I was rewarded with the sight of a school of 100 dolphins yesterday before dark. They were leaping 10 feet out of the water — amazing — so many at once! Had a squall last night, good wind, then it was light, did 3 knots. Now we are motoring for a few hours to try to get into the wind. Galápagos, we love you! 24 hour [run] — 89 miles.

◆ DEPARTURE: Leaving the Galápagos / Ecuador, beginning the long trade-wind crossing (~3,000 nm) to the Marquesas, French Polynesia.

### Galápagos → Marquesas (passage)

**Mon Apr 15**
Trade wind sailing — yeah! We picked up wind in a squall yesterday afternoon and it has stayed with us. Last night was beautiful, flat seas, sky blazing with stars, Charlotte charging along at 7 knots. We are still going strong in 15–18 knots south winds, forward of the beam, full sails, 6–7 knots constant. Saw Athanor last night just before sunset, lost [Tenia] finally. Ran the Coconut Breakfast net this morning — interesting to see how all the boats are spread out. Just about everyone has g[one] by now. 24 hour run — 138 miles.

**Tues Apr 16**
Yesterday was a great sailing day — hit over 8 knots at times, Charlotte really charging along. Unfortunately it changed today — variable winds, lumpy seas, speed up and down. Wish it would settle in again. Life proceeds on board — schooling takes up most of the day. No time to read anymore! 24 hour run 143 miles.

**Wed Apr 17**
Got into the groove last night — 55 miles in 7 hours! Today the wind turned more [aft] so we are poled out, consequently going slower. Thomas spotted 2 whales — we got to see just the wake, going very close to the boat. Trade wind weather today — blue sky, cumulus clouds, 15–20 knots. 24 hour run 148 miles.

**Thurs Apr 18**
Started getting some squalls with more punch to them, finally just leaving a reef in the main for now. Could use more sun, but otherwise no complaints, we are just rolling along. Thomas caught the world's record smallest dorado today. 24 hour run — 148 miles.

**Fri Apr 19**
Good wind today, 20 knots SE, reef in all the sails, 6.5–7 knots steady. A bit boisterous for head making! Caught a tuna just before dinner last night. 24 hour run — 165 miles. Thomas saw 2 pilot whales.

**Sat Apr 20**
The decks looked pressure washed after the torrential rains we had last night! Sails are clean too. It was no fun at the time — pole up, pole down, sails in and out. But this morning brought sunshine and calmer seas. The boys had a dinner dorado last night. There was a mayday report on the net this morning, at 4° 31.6S, 111° 48.7 W. [Bazen], [Marewa], and Athanor are diverting to check it out. We are currently sailing due west so we will be south of the position. Hope it's a false alarm! Winds are lighter today ± 10–12 ESE, making only 5+ knots. 24 hour run — 136 miles.

**Sun Apr 21**
Yesterday afternoon we set up 4 sails — poled out genoa, staysail, main, and mizzen — got them all flying on a very broad reach, reeled off the miles all night long. Took it down this morning when the wind picked up, but it was a nice rig. Saw a small school of dolphins just after sunset — they played in the bow wave for a long time, leaping into the air and doing back flips. Today is boisterous, 20+ knots, trade wind weather, rolling. 24 hour run — 151 miles. Celebrated 1/3 of the trip with a big bar of chocolate!

**Mon Apr 22**
Beautiful night last night — clear, moon, stars, no squalls. Got to catch up on our sleep. Today the wind has been very light, still moving around 5 knots or less. Back to school is difficult — it takes up a lot of time. 24 hours run — 142 miles.

**Tues Apr 23**
Cruising with our 4-sail trade wind rig again — 4 sails flying on a broad reach, 15 knots wind. Squalls slowed us down last night. Brian and I are having fun studying French. The ham radio nets are the high points of our day. 24-hour run — 138 miles.

**Wed Apr 24**
Nice night, beautiful half moon, no squalls. Today we passed the halfway point — will celebrate with spaghetti and red wine tonight. Wind light today, 10 knots, seas lumpy. 24 hour run — 147 miles.

**Thurs Apr 25**
Light winds, slow going, barely 5 knots under spinnaker. But Thomas did pull in a 10-lb yellow fin tuna, so they ate well for lunch and have dinner too. Sky completely clouded over now, wind up to 10[+] reaching but still only 5–5.5. It's a peaceful day however, everyone seems content. 24 hour run — 123 miles.

**Fri Apr 26**
Another slow but quiet night. It is very pretty at night with the moon almost full. You seem to see for miles. Kind of squally today, good sailing again, on a reach. 24 hour run — 126 miles.

**Sat Apr 27**
Great night's run — some squalls, had to reef down, but still moved along. 15–20 knots of wind, Charlotte is in her element! Beam beam (broad) reaching all day under full sail. Dropped under 1000 miles to go — moral[e] has improved! Enjoyed the sunshine today. 24-hour run — 151 miles.

**Sun Apr 28**
It was another beautiful night with the moon so bright. Great sailing last night, 6½ knots average. Today the winds are down, still doing 5 knots with the staysail up. Sunny day, comfortable on board. Listened in to another net this morning — so many boats out here! We have finally decided to make landfall at Hiva Oa. 24-hour run — 148 miles.

**Mon Apr 29**
Horrible night, rolled constantly, had weird dreams. Today is sloppy and slow — wind is almost north of east. We are running dead downwind. Must expect more of the same. 24 hour run — 126 miles.

**Tues Apr 30, 2002**
Calm night, no sail maneuvers necessary. Wind turned a bit south today so we are not quite downwind anymore, less rolly. Beautiful clear skies, no clouds. We now have an ETE on the GPS — less than 100 hours! 24-hour run — 135 miles.

**Wed May 1**
Happy May Day! Sharon on Poet's Place reminisced about flowers on May Day on the net this morning. Decent night, but the wind has been dropping all day. We are still moving along at 5 knots, incredibly enough. We want to get there! 24-hour run — 135 miles.

**Thurs May 2**
We just dropped under 300 miles to go. Unfortunately we still won't make it before dark on Saturday. Winds are 12–15 knots, very easterly, keep getting pushed south. Everyone else is having to tack back north to make their destination. 24-hour run — 133 miles.

**Fri May 3**
It was a really nice night — boat sailed smoothly, made good progress. This morning we talked to Warna Carina and Galatea on the radio and decided to change course once again. Now we are making for Nuku Hiva — we would have gotten to Hiva Oa at night. Plus the anchorage at Hiva Oa is small and uncomfortable, full of sharks. Another 60 miles, but we can make Nuku Hiva Sunday morning. We'll have to jibe as the course is now due west, impossible to steer. 24-hour run — 141 miles.

**Sat May 4**
This has been the longest day in the history of mankind! Moving along [in] 5 knots, feels like we are standing still. We want to get there now! Head downwind, rolling along. Nothing to do but read books and watch time pass slowly. 24-hour run — 121 miles.

### Marquesas, French Polynesia — Nuku Hiva (Taiohae), Daniel's Bay (Hakatea/Hakaui), Ua Pou (Hakahau, Hakahetau)

▸ STAY: ~2 weeks cruising the Marquesas (landfall ~May 5 at Nuku Hiva through ~May 20), moving among Taiohae, Daniel's Bay, and the villages of Ua Pou.

**Mon May 20**
What a wonderful 2 weeks in the Marquesas! Brian had a blast for a week with the Athanor kids — we hardly saw him. We took them all a couple of days. Started hiking up the mountain side on the road — paved switchbacks. Finally broke down and started hitchhiking. Got a ride with some locals going to collect bees. They dug a hive out of the side of a hill, smoked the bees out with coconut shell, we gorged on honey. The interior was very beautiful, different vegetation. Taiohae was one of the largest towns, were able to find some fruits and vegetables. Lots of boats in the harbor — met Dave & Gui on Galatea, great people. After Athanor left we cruised to Daniel's Bay, great landlocked harbor. Hiked to the waterfall with Galatea and Jack & Joyce from 2nd Tri. It was a magnificent walk through the valley, jagged cliffs looming over us. The waterfall was just a trickle, but Brian and I swam in the pool tucked into the rocks — kind of eerie! Daniel was a very sweet old man — gave us green coconuts to drink and we signed his book. The valley was paradise — flowers, fruit trees. The Marquesas can grow anything and the flowers are incredible — hibiscus, frangipani, bougainvillea, gardenia in a riot of colors. Also met the Japanese boat Ori Kalcum with Ken, Yuko, Yumi, Yuki on board — 12-year-old girl, very pretty, nice family, very different. We had a great time in Daniel's Bay, partied with Galatea. Then to Ua Pou and Hakahau, the main town. Visited the local museum there and learned about Marquesan history from Pascal. Then to Hakahetau, where Etienne lived. This was our favorite spot — the locals were so friendly to us. I got to practice my French — the Marquesans speak an easy version.

**(cont. — Hakahetau, Ua Pou)**
[Etienne] had a potluck for all the cruisers and everyone did songs or dances. Were invited to dinner by Jean-Marc, crazy Frenchman, had a wild time. Got to taste traditional Marquesan food. It was a beautiful little village. This morning they welcomed a cruise ship with singing & dancing, flower leis, food and handicrafts. Got a piece of flower stone and tou wood, both found only in Ua Pou. It was hard to leave, but the wind came back today so we are off to the Tuamotus. Idyllic stop in the Marquesas — we won't forget it. Wind SE, 15 knots, we are charging along under full sail.

◆ DEPARTURE: Leaving the Marquesas, bound SW for the Tuamotu atolls (still French Polynesia).

### Marquesas → Tuamotus (passage)

**Tues May 21**
Had a pleasant quiet night, steady wind but light. It got worse today! Down to less than 2 knots at times. Just set spinnaker, doing 3.3 now. Flat seas. Very comfortable on board but very hot. 24-hour run — 102 miles. (Will be much worse tomorrow!)

**Wed May 22**
Well half the night was nice, under spinnaker, 3–4 knots, but of course it didn't last! Went under a cloud line at 2am, SW winds, then no wind. Interrupted sleep, went nowhere. Total stall this morning, now we have 6 knots from the NE, under spinnaker again, 3.5 knots for a while but dropping now. Oh joy. All time slowest day! 24-hour run — 68 miles.

**Thurs May 23**
It's getting tiresome — rain & stall last night, very little wind today. We are tired of it. Now squalls from the SW, on the nose. Still too far to go. One good thing — Brian lost his last baby tooth today!! 24-hour run 63 miles.

**Fri May 24**
Well calm seas don't seem so bad when you are battling 35 knots on the nose! The wind came up at 5pm last night and kept coming. Couldn't keep the course at first. Saw a sailboat just at dusk. It was a wild night, Charlotte falling into holes, had to dump the main completely. No sleep, too rough. Wind has been letting off and backing throughout the day, sailing rather pleasantly now, reasonably upright. Didn't do anything all day but lie around and read. 24-hour run 103 miles.

**Sat May 25**
Nice night, but the winds kept turning south. We can't hold our course today, have to motor sail to avoid hitting atolls! Hope the wind will turn more favorable this evening, we have to hang out all night and wait for the morning's slack tide. We are tired of this trip! Could sail just fine, in another direction.

### Tuamotus, French Polynesia — Fakarava atoll

▸ STAY: ~2½ weeks at Fakarava (landfall Sun May 26 through departure Tues June 11), anchored mainly at the south pass near Motu Aito.

**Tues June 11**
It was with regrets that we left Fakarava this morning — we had such a wonderful time there and made so many friends. Some of them are already in Papeete and we will see them there. Athanor stayed another week in Fakarava and the kids played together every day, exploring the motus. Infidien was there with Rick, Patty and Jessica, and we got to be friends.
